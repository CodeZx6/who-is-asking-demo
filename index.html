Who Is Asking?Zero-Shot Query Provenance Verification for Retrieval-Augmented Voice Agents

Anonymous authors

Reception robotQuery galleryScore distributions

Abstract

Retrieval-augmented voice agents search the Web for the people who speak to them through phones, smart displays, meeting rooms, and reception robots. A typed query carries the account of its issuer and a spoken query does not, so before it retrieves or acts the agent has to verify that the query came from its user and not from a bystander, in every new room, microphone, and camera, without labels, enrollment, or tuning. However, adaptive retrieval methods judge a query by its transcript and the uncertainty of the language model, so they cannot see who is speaking, and audio-visual speaker models learn scores defined only up to a scenario-dependent offset, so they need a threshold calibrated in each scenario. Moreover, we show that a change of speaker alters only the correspondence between voice and face, so any statistic of one modality is invariant to it, whereas a change of scenario moves the distributions and preserves the correspondence, so the pointwise mutual information between face and speech changes sign with the speaker in every scenario. To exploit this property, we propose FaceVouch, a zero-shot verifier of query provenance composed of a pointwise mutual information estimator and a sign-based decision rule. In the estimator, one frozen audio-visual speech model predicts masked speech units with and without the face, and the contrast of the two log-likelihoods cancels how predictable the speech is in each scenario. In the decision rule, the sign of the averaged estimate decides whether the query is accepted, enabling a fixed threshold at zero without calibration. Experiments on studio conversations and on meetings with headset and far-field microphones demonstrate that FaceVouch attains the highest discrimination across scenarios under one fixed rule, blocks nearly all bystander queries and most spoken instructions of a bystander while preserving the retrieval quality of the user, and transfers unchanged to a reception robot.

Reception robot

Held-out test queries are replayed to a humanoid reception robot. The verifier's decisions and the agent's answers are cached from the experiments, so in each scene the two videos differ only in whether the verifier is applied. Each video shows what the robot sees and hears (who is in view, who is speaking, what is said), the verifier's decision, and how the robot reacts; the waits between queries are cut.
